CS foundations: mechanisms & connections

I follow the path from data organization and instruction execution to resource management and host communication, connecting mechanisms that appear across different chapters.

Data structures

Structure determines cost

Arrays provide O(1) random access and good locality; positional access in a linked list is O(n). Hash-table lookup is O(1) on average but O(n) in the worst case, while a balanced search tree preserves ordered queries in O(log n).

Computer organization

Translation and caching

A TLB caches page-table entries to reduce address-translation cost, while caches transfer blocks to exploit temporal and spatial locality. A cache miss accesses a lower cache or main memory; disk is involved only when an invalid page-table entry corresponds to a page that is not resident.

Operating systems

Concurrency and resource constraints

A mutex protects a critical section; a semaphore can also represent resource counts and ordering. Deadlock becomes possible when mutual exclusion, hold-and-wait, no preemption, and circular wait all hold.

Networks

Reliability and congestion control

TCP uses sequence numbers, acknowledgments, and retransmission for reliability, with a sliding window limiting unacknowledged data. The send limit is the smaller of the receiver window and the congestion window.

Mathematics

Conditions, derivation & geometric intuition

I first identify a formula’s assumptions and geometric object, then test the conclusion through derivation, region analysis, and counterexamples.

Calculus

Gradients and local change

For a unit direction v, the directional derivative is Dᵥf = ∇fᵀv; the gradient gives the direction of steepest ascent, and the Hessian describes local curvature. Optimization directions and step sizes depend on this local information.

Linear algebra

Operators and frequency domain

Blur can be written as a linear operator H, and the data-term gradient contains Hᵀ(Hx-y). Under periodic boundaries, the DFT diagonalizes circular convolution, turning a linear solve into elementwise division by frequency.

Probability

Noise and data fidelity

The negative log-likelihood of independent Gaussian noise yields an L2 data term; Laplace noise yields L1. The loss should follow from the noise assumption rather than habit.

Optimization

Nonsmooth terms and splitting

Proximal operators handle nonsmooth regularizers such as L1 and TV. ADMM alternates after variable splitting; Primal–Dual methods iterate between primal and dual variables under step-size conditions.

Image restoration

Models, priors & experimental evidence

I first specify how a clean image becomes an observation, then compare how optimization priors and deep models use that degradation process to reconstruct the image.

Degradation
y = DBx + n: B is blur, D is downsampling, and n is noise. Training and testing must use consistent kernel centers, boundary rules, and numerical ranges.
Explicit prior
TV promotes sparse image gradients, suppressing noise while retaining major edges; excessive regularization creates staircasing and removes texture.
Learned prior
CNNs use local receptive fields and hierarchical features, while Transformers model longer-range dependencies. Both remain sensitive to mismatch between training degradations and test inputs.
Evaluation
PSNR measures pixel error and SSIM compares local structure. Neither replaces visual inspection, and neither should be compared across different datasets or degradation settings.
Cross-topic links

Across subjects, through the stack

A single problem often crosses several courses. These three links place concepts back into a complete execution path instead of treating each chapter in isolation.

From access patterns to storage
A data structure determines access order and locality. Locality then affects cache hits, TLB behavior, and page faults; at larger scales, file organization and disk I/O also become part of the cost.
From a function call to I/O completion
An application I/O request passes through a library function, system call, device-independent layer, and driver. With interrupt-driven I/O, the device reports completion by interrupt; DMA can move blocks of data, but the processor still configures the transfer and handles its completion notification.
From degradation to optimization
Convolution expresses blur as a linear operator, Fourier transforms simplify solving under periodic boundaries, probability models describe noise, and gradients, convexity, and duality support the restoration algorithm.
