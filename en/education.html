Education

Learning Path.

My undergraduate work extends beyond coursework to paper reproduction, experiment debugging, engineering organization, and continued review.

Timeline

Learning path.

I build structure from fundamentals, use real projects to expose problems, and return to first principles to explain the results.

Foundation

Computer science and mathematics

Systematic study of data structures, computer organization, operating systems, and networks, supported by calculus, linear algebra, and probability.

Research

Image restoration and deep learning

Starting from convolution, frequency analysis, and regularization, then extending to CNNs, Transformers, Plug-and-Play, and Primal–Dual methods.

Practice

Paper reproduction and experimental validation

Translating degradation models, loss functions, and inference steps from papers into code, then checking them with metrics, visual results, and ablation thinking.

Engineering

Maintainable implementation

Managing dependencies, data, logs, checkpoints, GPU memory, and documentation so experiments can be rerun, compared, and extended.

Method

My learning process.

Each stage preserves inspectable intermediate results instead of only final conclusions.

01

Define conceptual boundaries

Define objects, conditions, and conclusions; distinguish similar concepts; and connect prerequisites with later use.

02

Derive and verify

Keep the essential derivation of formulas and algorithms, then verify each step with representative problems.

03

Expose details through code

Use implementation to reveal omitted boundary conditions, data formats, numerical ranges, and computational costs.

04

Use results to revisit principles

When metrics fail or NaNs, color shifts, or OOM errors appear, trace inputs, models, optimizers, and inference stages systematically.
