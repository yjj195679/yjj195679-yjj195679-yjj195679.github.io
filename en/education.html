Learning method

How I learn and verify

I begin with definitions and assumptions, then use derivations, code, and experiments to test my understanding. When something fails, I trace the symptom back through the relevant stages.

Study sequence

How the subjects connect

Computer science and mathematics provide the analytical tools, image-restoration papers provide concrete problems, and code and experiments test the derivations and assumptions.

Foundation

Computer science and mathematics

I review data structures, computer organization, operating systems, and networks while studying calculus, linear algebra, and probability as foundations for algorithm analysis and model derivation.

Research

Image restoration and deep learning

I start with convolution, frequency analysis, degradation models, and regularization, then study the distinct roles of CNNs, Transformers, Plug-and-Play, and Primal–Dual methods.

Practice

Paper reproduction and experimental validation

I translate each degradation model, loss function, and inference step into code, then check the implementation with training logs, PSNR / SSIM, and output images.

Engineering

Maintainable implementation

I record dependencies, data paths, training logs, checkpoints, and GPU-memory use so an experiment can be rerun and compared directly after a change.

Study steps

Four steps I use in practice

I use these steps for mathematics, CS exam topics, and project work. The goal is to explain why a conclusion holds and under which conditions it applies.

01

Define conceptual boundaries

State the object, given conditions, and required conclusion first. Then compare similar concepts to avoid applying a formula outside its valid conditions.

02

Preserve the decisive steps

Keep every step that determines why a formula or algorithm is valid. If a step is omitted, I should still be able to name the definition, theorem, or assumption it uses.

03

Expose details through code

After translating a formula into code, check boundary conditions, tensor shapes, numerical ranges, and computational cost—the details that often determine whether the implementation works.

04

Trace results back to causes

When metrics fail or NaNs, color shifts, or OOM errors appear, inspect the input, model output, loss and optimization process, and inference settings until the failure can be reproduced and explained.
